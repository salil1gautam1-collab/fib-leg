Entry and Stop are always the zone — entry at the 0.5–0.618 area (the 5m nested fib refines the exact fill), stop a 0.786 close (hedge-covered) — those two are fixed by the validated system, so their selectors are hidden. You choose Exit and Trigger. All "let it run" modes scale out ⅓ at entry-dependent targets (the 1/d harmonic projection) and ratchet the stop up as each target hits. Square all at T1 = take it all off at 0.95, no runner. Let it run + BE = after T1 the stop goes to breakeven. Let it run + lock B = after T1 the stop locks at B (the T1 price), so a failed continuation keeps the B-level profit instead of giving it back — the validated best exit on the 11-yr walk-forward. Trigger is the interval a close beyond the stop (or a target) must occur on — 5m or 15m. History + stats update so you can A/B exit × trigger.

Setup filter

Every mode enters at the zone (0.5–0.618 + nested fib, 0.786 stop) — this filter just picks which setups you see. ⭐ Best (default) = the market-context gate: India VIX calm, the stock's sector trending with the trade, Nifty not in the ADX-20–25 whipsaw, and projected reward ≥ risk (R:R ≥ 1) — the 11-yr walk-forward's best-of-the-best set (+183R on a third of the trades, drawdown roughly halved). Reversal + trend = an M/W or pin-bar reversal at the leg origin AND higher-timeframe agreement. All = every zone setup (+172.8R — nearly the same: once the leg is drawn correctly the filters add little; the edge is the leg, not the filter). A+ = a prior support/resistance level sitting near the 0.5–0.618 zone (fib + level = "double confidence") — a modest add-on, not a hard requirement. Only M/W = leg origin has a double top/bottom (a W starts an up-impulse → long; an M starts a down-impulse → short). Only Pin = a clean pin bar (hammer / shooting star) at the origin. When a setup has no mountain/valley it still enters at the plain 0.5–0.618 zone and the chart flags it "⚠ no mountain/valley." If price gaps over the zone or never retraces to it, there's no clean entry, so the setup is skipped (no chasing — keeps losses small).

❓ What does R mean? (tap to expand)

R = the money you agreed to lose if the trade goes wrong. Example: buy at ₹790 with the stop at ₹766 → risk ₹24/share; with 950 shares (one lot) that's ₹22,800 on the line. That amount = 1R for this trade. Every result is then measured in that unit: stop hit = −1R (−₹22,800) · a winner that makes double the risk = +2R (+₹45,600).

Why not just rupees? Every trade risks a different amount, so rupee totals hide whether it was skill or one oversized bet. Converting each trade to "profit ÷ risk taken" makes them all comparable — like grading every exam out of 100.

So "+151R over 10 years" means: if you risked the same amount on every trade, you'd end with 151 × that amount in profit. And "1% risk per trade" means sizing positions so 1R = 1% of your account — a losing trade stings but can never knock you out; that sizing is what the compound (×) and CAGR numbers below assume.

One caution: R is risk-relative. A tight stop makes each R smaller in rupees, a wide stop bigger — which is why the agent sizes every trade from your capital and risk %, not from a fixed share count.
