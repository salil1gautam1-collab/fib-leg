❓ What does R mean? (tap to expand)

R = the money you agreed to lose if the trade goes wrong. Example: buy at ₹790 with the stop at ₹766 → risk ₹24/share; with 950 shares (one lot) that's ₹22,800 on the line. That amount = 1R for this trade. Every result is then measured in that unit: stop hit = −1R (−₹22,800) · a winner that makes double the risk = +2R (+₹45,600).

Why not just rupees? Every trade risks a different amount, so rupee totals hide whether it was skill or one oversized bet. Converting each trade to "profit ÷ risk taken" makes them all comparable — like grading every exam out of 100.

So "+151R over 10 years" means: if you risked the same amount on every trade, you'd end with 151 × that amount in profit. And "1% risk per trade" means sizing positions so 1R = 1% of your account — a losing trade stings but can never knock you out; that sizing is what the compound (×) and CAGR numbers below assume.

One caution: R is risk-relative. A tight stop makes each R smaller in rupees, a wide stop bigger — which is why the agent sizes every trade from your capital and risk %, not from a fixed share count.

🎲 Gamma explained — step by step (with pictures)

Read top to bottom — each step builds on the last. Plain words, no maths.

Step 1 — Why it happens. Big banks ("the shop") quietly buy and sell real shares to stay safe on option bets. They're so huge that this buying and selling nudges the price.

Step 2 — That nudge does one of two opposite things:

Step 3 — Three words: 🧱 Wall = the magnet price (where the most bets pile up). 🥣 Sticky = price stuck to the wall. ⛰️ Runs = price breaks away.

Step 4 — It works BOTH ways. The push isn't locked to up or down — it's locked to IN or OUT:

🥣 Stickypushes IN, toward the wall — down if price is above it, up if below
⛰️ Runspushes OUT, away from the middle — up if rising, down if falling

So the word that flips isn't up/down — it's IN vs OUT. Which of up or down that becomes just depends on where price is sitting right now.

Step 5 — One line decides which world you're in: the flip. Above it = sticky, below it = runs. Cross it and the world switches:

Step 6 — How the flip is found (a tug-of-war). Call bets pull toward 🥣 sticky, put bets pull toward ⛰️ running. Add up both pulls at a price; the price where they're exactly tied is the flip. Above it calls win (sticky), below it puts win (running). (This rests on one assumption — that dealers are long calls / short puts. If that's wrong, the flip is wrong — which is exactly why the engine has to prove itself forward, one live day at a time.)

Step 7 — What the 🎲 robot does. It asks two questions — "sticky or runs?" and "where's price vs the wall?" — then bets:

  • Sticky + price above the wall → bet it comes down to the wall.
  • Sticky + price below the wall → bet it goes up to the wall.
  • Runs, breaking up → bet up; breaking down → bet down.

Step 8 — How we'll know if it works. The robot has its own ₹10 lakh at 1% risk — ₹10,000 per trade, sized so every signal it sees is tradable. If that grows over the coming weeks, gamma pays; if not, it doesn't. That's the whole answer. Since 15 Jul it also checks the weather before staking a sticky trade — see 🌦 below. (Official names, if you ever read about it: sticky = "pinning", running = "gamma squeeze".)

📗 The two books — trade book vs shadow book

Every engine writes into two ledgers, and the difference matters more than anything else on this site.

The trade book is the scorecard — the trades the engine actually stakes its (paper) ₹10 lakh on. When we ask "is this engine any good?", the trade book answers.

The shadow book is the recorder. When a safety rule blocks a trade, the trade is NOT thrown away — it runs in the shadow book from entry to exit, exactly as it would have. So every rule carries its own scorecard: if the trades it blocked keep losing, that rule's ⛔ line reads "the gate is saving money ✓"; if they quietly turn profitable, it reads "consider reverting" and the rule is removed. No rule survives on opinion.

One phrase we never mix up: "real money" means the live phase — months away, and only if this paper audition passes. Everything on this site, both books, is paper.

🌦 The weather rule (added 15 Jul 2026)

The story. Three losing sessions in a row (−5.5R, −6.5R, −15.6R) — and every one happened while the market was either ⛰️ running or nervous (VIX high). On quiet sideways days the same engine was making money. Same trades, different weather, opposite results.

The rule. A sticky trade now earns a place in the trade book only when the weather is quiet — market sideways AND VIX calm. In any other weather it still fires, but into the shadow book:

🥣 sideways + VIX calmtrade book ✓
🥣 sideways + VIX highshadow — tag vix-high (nervous chop hits stop after stop)
⛰️ running (any VIX)shadow — tag runs-aligned or counter-run (walls don't hold)

What counts as "VIX high"? (tightened 16 Jul 2026) VIX must be above its own 20-day average and above 15. The first version used only the average — and on a dead-calm day (VIX ~12.9 vs average ~13.0) the flag flickered high/calm on 3-paise ticks. Below 15 the market simply isn't nervous, whatever the average says. Every fill still records the raw VIX, so the review can re-score both definitions.

Plus a circuit breaker. If the trade book loses 5R in one day it stops for the day — every later fill routes to shadow (tag: day-breaker). That is protection against the oldest trap in trading: re-entering all afternoon hoping to win it back.

The honest part: this rule would also have blocked the single best trade so far (+20.6R on a wild day). The shadow book is now recording exactly what the rule saves and what it costs — in a few weeks its ⛔ lines will say whether it stays.

What Defense taught us (16 Jul 2026 — tested three ways, left alone). The same weather question was asked of 🛡 Defense, three times, with three different machines: a fixed gate (costs 2.8R for every 1R it saves), a self-adjusting gate at every memory length (the quarter-memory version flipped 91 times and did worse than never waking), and a two-key version armed by real FII positioning data. Every version made less money than leaving Defense alone — because two-thirds of its big winners are BORN on wild days. Defense trades untouched; the Book absorbs its storm years (other engines cover them). The two-key idea survives for engines whose wild-day trades actually lose.

📈 The positioning recorder (16 Jul 2026). Every day the system now records the market's "stress gauges": India VIX, put/call OI balance, dealer gamma, distance to the flip, and FII index-futures positioning. An 11-year check showed a sharp one-day FII sell-down lifts tomorrow's storm odds from 62% to 76% — a real, modest early-warning. Nothing trades off it yet: it builds the evidence for smarter gate timing, and the 🎲 tab shows today's reading.

The gate that adjusts itself (⚡ Scalper, added 16 Jul 2026). The Scalper's 1H 0.618 trades got the same weather rule — but adaptive. An 11-year replay showed a clean era break: hostile-weather 0.618s earned +332R in 2015–2022, then lost −129R from 2023 on. So instead of a fixed stance, this gate reads its own scorecard: hostile-weather trades are benched to the shadow book while their trailing 12-month record is negative, and re-admitted automatically once the shadow book shows them clearly profitable again. Walk-forward, that self-adjusting rule beat both fixed choices (+825R vs +799R always-take / +467R never-take) with only ~1 flip a year. The 🌦 line in the 📒 Trades tab shows its current stance and the running evidence.

What is R?

1R = the rupees you agreed to lose on one trade (entry to stop, times size). Every result is measured in it: +2R = won twice what you risked; −1R = one full stop. R makes trades comparable across a ₹90 stock and a ₹9,000 stock. Careful when mixing systems: a scalp R and a swing R are different rupee amounts unless sizing makes them equal — the Book's forced sizing does exactly that (1% of the book's equity per trade, so 1R is the same ₹ in every engine).

Costs: what 0.05R vs 0.10R means, and who controls it

The toll a round trip always takes, win or lose, as a slice of your risked money: 0.05R = 5 paise per rupee risked · 0.10R = 10 paise. With 1R ≈ ₹1,125 that's ₹56 vs ₹112 per trade. Multiplied by ~500 trades/yr: roughly 6% vs 12.5% of the account per year in tolls — the Scalper's whole edge lives inside that gap.

Who owns each piece: brokerage (₹20/order, broker's — already minimal) · taxes (government's, fixed) · the spread — the market offers it, but YOU choose whether to pay it. Two decisions put you in the cheap tier: (1) only liquid strikes, spread ≤1–2% of premium, entered with resting limits and exited with limit targets — only stop-outs ever cross the spread; (2) a bigger R dilutes the fixed ₹70-ish of charges. The broker cannot discount you into the good tier; your fills do.

The spread — why "premium + profit" isn't automatic

There is never one price on the screen — there's the ask (you buy here) and the bid (you sell here). Fair value sits between. Buy at 20.20 with the bid at 19.80 and you are down ₹0.40/share instantly, with intrinsic and time value unchanged. A swing spreads that toll over a ₹40 move; a scalper plays for ₹3–8 moves and feels every paisa. Index options: gap ≈ ₹0.05 (nearly free). Illiquid stock options: ₹0.50+ (the whole edge). That's the entire cost-tier story in one quote box.

The fib lifecycle (the law all engines obey)

An impulse is born when a move breaks a mountain/valley. A close through 0.382 confirms the top — the fib is FINALIZED, levels frozen. The origin re-anchors only if the pullback closed past 0.618 (gray zone between 0.382 and 0.618 + a new high = same origin, the institutions defended — the 4H referees any doubt on the 2H). A fib dies exactly two ways: a 5-minute close past the 0.886 cushion (walk away) or a close past its own high (new impulse). Wicks never break anything. Down-moves before the 0.886 breaks are the delivery vans — they bring price to our levels; that's what retracements are.

Why shorts were cut (the 2022 investigation)

"Why was 2022 a −15.8R year?" peeled the onion: the short side had lost money in 9 of 12 years, −61R total, hidden inside good-looking combined totals — the long side was quietly subsidizing it. 2022 just removed the camouflage (longs +11.4 couldn't cover shorts −27.2). Every rescue was tested and failed out-of-sample: index regime veto (aligned shorts were the WORST: −32R), capitulation gates, faster exits (65% of losing shorts never even saw +0.5R — entries were wrong, not exits), deep levels with honest stops. The honest trade: cutting shorts costs ~34R in 2015+2018 (the two Nifty-correction years) and pays ~+95R everywhere else. The only surviving shorts: the 💎 Gem's (index 0.886, both sides, tiny sample, on probation).

Resting orders vs acting on confirmation

Two ordering methods, both in the Book: Pocket acts on confirmation (close into the zone → close back out → nested 5m fill) — the swing way, no resting orders. Scalper and Defense rest limit orders at the level — filled at the first clean touch (approach side, no gap fills), which is why they pay no spread going in. The research verdict: at deep levels held for days, resting orders earned ~3.5× the confirmation version — the deep edge lives in the fill AT the level. When the same 1H 0.786 touch fills both Scalper (30-min trade) and Defense (10-day trade), BOTH stand — different businesses on one event; the ledger tags it ⚠both-books and the stock counts twice against the risk ceiling.

Stops: the hunting ground

Measured over 11 years: before the real turn, raids below 0.786 run ~0.35–0.45% typically, ~0.9% at the 75th percentile, ~1.5% deep; below 0.886 half that (~0.45% at P75) — the 0.886 is the most precisely defended line on the chart. Swing stops belong BEYOND the raid zone. The Defense book deliberately does the opposite: tight stops inside the raid zone, paying frequent cheap stop-outs as rent while survivors run for days — cut losses fast, let winners run, mechanized. Live stops: triggered off the UNDERLYING (never option quotes), a protective order also rests at the broker, exits escalate limit → aggressive limit → market, and a bought option's max loss is the premium — the instrument itself caps disasters.

Who starts the engines, and when you are needed

Pocket has a Start button because you set its capital, risk and start date. Scalper, Gem and Defense never sleep — cloud books with fixed forced sizing on virtual ₹10L each (full-coverage paper sizing); they started at deployment and run every trading day. You are needed at exactly four moments: (1) money in/out, (2) changing the rules themselves, (3) restarting after a tripwire halt — the machine stops itself and never talks itself back in, (4) occasional admin. Routine sizing never needs you: everything inside the rules is the machine's; everything that changes, funds, or resumes the rules is the owner's.

How a strategy retires (its own fib levels)

A strategy is a position too — it gets a stop-loss defined before entry. The 11-year history defines normal suffering (worst drawdown, streaks, flat years). At ~75% of the worst-ever drawdown the agent halves size (the strategy's 0.618 — sellers might be winning). Beyond the worst-ever (its 0.886) it halts completely — this is outside anything the living edge ever produced. Then it keeps trading on PAPER while real money sits out: shadow recovery = documented case to restart; continued bleeding = retirement confirmed. The machine gathers the verdict; only the owner executes it.

The ladder to real money

Backtest → paper on delayed data (now) → paper on Fyers real-time (costs and fills verified) → small real size → scale. Each rung can veto the next. The backtests are tests on the past, not promises — the paper books' one job is to check the Book's yearly table against reality before a single rupee moves. 2026 was soft in most backtest variants: one more reason paper goes first.
